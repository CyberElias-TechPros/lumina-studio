## 0–10 · Review & warm-up

Project a 9-second ad; freezes at 0:03; room finishes the story by asking "what happens next?" — the curiosity engine. Every edit feeds it or starves it.

## 10–25 · Teaching block

**Story shapes.** The 3-act micro: hook (problem/promise, ≤2s) → development (steps/proofs/scenes) → payoff (result/CTA).
**Short-form** wants hook-in-1-second + loopable ending. **Long-form** still tolerates one slow beat — never two.
**Footage triage.** Before timeline: watch EVERYTHING once at 2×, tag keeps (⭐), maybes (?), kills (✕). The editor who starts timelines blind edits forever.
**Assembly edit.** Dump keeps in scene order, no trimming yet; watch start-to-finish; find the true start (stab at the hook) and the true end; only then touch a razor.

## 25–45 · Guided demonstration

Projected raw dump (14 clips, unsorted): instructor triages with keyboard tags while narrating; builds assembly in 8 minutes; identifies the misplaced "perfect" clip hiding at 4:20; exports the 60-second skeleton to timeline.

## 45–70 · Guided practice

Pairs: triage a shared 10-clip dump on their machines; assemble one story from a brief card ("customer pain → chef solves in 90 seconds"); swap timelines; partner finds one dead second that must die.

## 70–95 · Independent practice

Solo: assembly edit of YOUR raw dump (two files: the vertical piece and the horizontal one) — no trimming past the hook cuts yet; log the triage counts. File to workbook.

## 95–110 · Correction & check

KC1: (1) 3-act micro shape in seconds. (2) Short-form hook window. (3) Triage tag meanings. (4) Assembly edit rule (no ___ yet). (5) Find the true start — what are you hunting?

## 110–120 · Summary & next session

Order before razor. Next: the cut — rhythm and action.

## Answers — KC1

1. Hook ≤2s → development (steps/proof) → payoff + CTA. 2) ≤1 second. 3) ⭐ keep, ? maybe, ✕ kill. 4) No trimming beyond the hook-to-assembly skeleton — structure before style. 5) The first frame that compels; everything before it is throat-clearing.
