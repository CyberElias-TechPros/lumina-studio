## 0–10 · Review & warm-up

Projected flyer A (cramped, 7 fonts) and B (airy, 2 fonts) — room rates instantly; discuss what made their eye relax.

## 10–25 · Teaching block

**Layout is decision order.** What should eyes hit 1st, 2nd, 3rd? Hierarchy answers that with size/weight/colour/space. A design without hierarchy is a shouting match in a quiet library.

**Alignment & balance.** Everything snaps to an invisible line — edges, centres, baselines. Balance: symmetric = formal/calm; asymmetric = energetic/editorial; either is fine, half-each is chaos.
Contrast: size, weight, dark/light, warm/cool — you need ONE dominant contrast + supporting cast.
**White space is not empty.** It is the silence that makes the sentence land. Beginners drown layouts; professionals salt them.
**Visual communication ethics.** Design persuades — it must carry the truth of the offer; a flyer promising what the shop can't deliver is a lie with good kerning.

## 25–45 · Guided demonstration

Instructor fixes a projected disaster live: 7 fonts → 2; centre-everything → grid; 3 competing headlines → one headline + subline; remove the glitter; readability test at 3 metres (a poster passes or it's decoration).

## 45–70 · Guided practice

Pairs: fifteen-layout critique gauntlet — browse 15 printed flyers on the tables; tag each GOOD/BROKEN with one reason (hierarchy, alignment, contrast, spacing); compare with key sheet; two "why" debates aloud.

## 70–95 · Independent practice

Solo: **flyer autopsy** — bring/photograph one dirty flyer from your bag; mark the five hierarchy fixes you'd make in red pen; file to workbook.

## 95–110 · Correction & check

KC1: (1) Hierarchy = what question answered? (2) Two balance types + example. (3) White space's job. (4) Contrast types (any three). (5) 3-metre poster test.

## 110–120 · Summary & next session

Eyes before hands. Next: colour that behaves and type that reads.

## Answers — KC1

1. Where does the eye go first/second/third? 2) Symmetric (wedding invite formality) vs asymmetric (editorial energy). 3) Silence before the sentence — relief, focus, premium feel. 4) Size, weight, dark/light, warm/cool. 5) If it can't be read from 3m, it's decoration, not a poster.
