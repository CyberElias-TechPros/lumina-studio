## 0–10 · Review & warm-up

Room votes: "Name one person whose work you'd pay for today" — five answers written on board.
Pattern spotting: every answer has an outcome, a number, and a timeline.

## 10–25 · Teaching block

**Skills vs services.** Skill = what you can do (weld, write, wire, bake, shoot video). Service = a
bought outcome: "WordPress site for a caterer, 10 pages, live in 3 weeks, ₦450,000." Clients never
buy skills; they buy outcomes with predictable risk.

**The service formula:** WHO (specific audience) + WHAT (concrete deliverable) + HOW LONG (time) +
HOW MUCH (price) + REVISIONS (included). Vague offers die unread.

**Finding useful problems.** List 20 problems people in your circle pay for; star the 5 you can solve
in 2–4 weeks; pick 1 as your lead service.

**Pricing concept preview.** Your price = income target ÷ billable hours; never below your cost floor.
(Deep-dive in S4.)

**Branding & portfolio preview.** Brand = what people say about you when you're not in the room. Portfolio
= proof: 3 case studies, before/after/result.

## 25–45 · Guided demonstration

Instructor converts "I do typing" → "I format academic project work: A4, bound, 72 hours, ₦10,000,
two revision rounds" live on board; class iterates three more examples (tailoring, phone repair, lessons).

## 45–70 · Guided practice

Pairs: each takes one skill from the board → WHO/WHAT/WHEN/HOW MUCH/REVISIONS table on paper; swap;
ochred by partner for vagueness (challenge: "could this be pasted into a WhatsApp message unchanged?").

## 70–95 · Independent practice

Solo: your **three services one-pager** — three offers from YOUR skill, each with the full formula,
one audience named, one price guessed (defended later in S4). File to workbook.

## 95–110 · Correction & check

KC1: (1) Skill vs service — example each. (2) Three parts of a concrete offer. (3) Why named audiences beat "everybody". (4) What is a case study vs a screenshot? (5) Two revisions included — why write it down?
Self-mark.

## 110–120 · Summary & next session

Offers, not intentions. Next: finding people ethically and getting replies.

## Answers — KC1

1. Skill = capability; service = purchasable outcome. 2) Deliverable, timeline, price (plus audience/revisions). 3) Specific audiences have specific pains — generic offers get ignored. 4) Case study = problem→approach→result narrative; screenshot = unverifiable clippings. 5) Scope protection and expectation-setting — verbal agreements evaporate.

## Homework (15 min)

Spy on one competitor's service page; note their WHO/WHAT/HOW MUCH.
