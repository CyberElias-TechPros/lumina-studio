## 0–10 · Review & warm-up

Room reset; pitch order drawn; peer-challenge cards printed ("why is this price?", "who exactly buys this?", "what if it rains on delivery day?").

## 10–25 · Teaching block

**One page, decision-ready.** WHO (named 1–2 audience archetypes) · OFFER (your three services with prices) ·
PACKAGES (Basic/Standard/Premium) · HOW YOU FIND THEM (warm circle → referrals → marketplace; this week's one action)
· MONEY (breakeven, deposit rule, ledger habit) · REPUTATION (the one promise you never break).

**Case study anatomy (per piece):** Client & problem · constraints · approach (3 steps) · result (numbers if any)
· testimonial (if any). Three real (or practice-grade fictional-but-honest) pieces. No screenshots alone.

**Defending the price to the class.** Expect: "why not half?" — answer with cost floor + value + market range.
"why not double?" — know your ceiling; concede if you don't yet know. Under-proving is better than over-proving.

## 25–45 · Final project (individual work)

Solo (observed, 25 min): one-page plan finalised · three case studies drafted — from YOUR skill, actual work
or plausible practice work, honest about which. Instructor files to cohort folder.

## 45–75 · Class pitches

Each student: 3 minutes — who sells, to whom, at what price, how they find the first five. Room uses challenge cards;
class votes "would you buy?" honestly. Instructor scores P-gate sheet.

## 75–100 · Peer critique & pricing challenge

Pairs swap plans: find weakest section; one pair presents best/worst price defence to class. Instructor moderates;
no mockery; the pricing muscle is built here.

## 100–110 · Correction & check

KC6: (1) Six parts of the one-page plan. (2) Case study, three elements. (3) Your cost floor defended in one line. (4) First five clients — your named list. (5) What peer critique is for.

## 110–120 · Course close

Certificate gate: plan passes the "sell tomorrow" test. Then — practice: send the first proposal TODAY, not Sunday.
Alumni pathway: digital marketing for acquisition, data analytics for the numbers, online teaching to teach your craft.

## Answers — KC6

1. Who · offer · packages · acquisition · money · reputation. 2) Problem · approach · result (numbers + testimonial if any). 3) Cost floor = costs ÷ margin; below it you pay the client to work. 4) Named warm-circle five — written down, not remembered. 5) Pressure-testing, not judgement; the market is harsher than friends.

## Homework

None. Send that first proposal within 72 hours.
