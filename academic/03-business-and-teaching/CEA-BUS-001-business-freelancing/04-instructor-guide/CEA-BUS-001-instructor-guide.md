**Do not distribute.** Keys in Section 7.

## 1. What this course is

Craft-to-cash transfer. Students arrive with skill and fear of pricing; leave with a one-page plan,
three case studies and a ledger. Economics matter more than rhetoric; keep the room honest.

## 2. Preparation

Printed proposal/price/kick-off/ledger templates; role-play cards (client briefs, complaints);
challenge cards for pricing defence; the messy practice ledger file; receipts jar prop for S5 demo.

## 3. Delivery model

105 min: 0–10 board question · 10–25 teach · 25–45 demo/role-play · 45–70 guided · 70–95 solo/pitch · 95–110 KC · 110–120 close.

## 4. Failure points

- S1: vague offers ("I help businesses grow") — enforce the WHO/WHAT/WHEN/HOW MUCH formula every time.
- S2: proposal before discovery — if the sheet has no questions asked, the proposal gets no price; redo.
- S3: "over-servicing to please" — call it scope creep with a smile; the written sentence is the muscle.
- S4: pricing by "how much would you pay ME?" — walk them through cost floor first, always.
- S6: gate = real, specific offer; generic "Nigerian brands" audiences fail the test.

## 5. Assessment guidance

A1 (packages): deliverables listed, revision policy stated, timeline, deposit line. Final: plan P1–P6
(sell-tomorrow gate), three case studies, observed pitch; show-and-tell = price defence reasoning.
No credit for glossiness without substance.

## 6. Equity & access

Low-literacy learners can use voice-notes templates; print all charts; pair strengths in role-plays;
no student forced to disclose real income numbers.

## 7. Knowledge-check keys

KC1: 1) Capability vs purchasable outcome. 2) Deliverable, timeline, price. 3) Specific audiences carry specific pains. 4) Story vs clippings. 5) Scope protection.
KC2: 1) Warm circle announce once. 2) Any three discovery Qs. 3) 8 sections: understanding, approach, deliverables, timeline, price, terms, validity, next step. 4) Discovery reveals real problem/budget. 5) Who + fix + next step, 3 lines.
KC3: 1) Scope restated, dates, their inputs deadline, revisions, milestones, channel. 2) "Proceed with A unless I hear otherwise by tomorrow." 3) Add + price + days + confirm in writing. 4) Acknowledge → investigate → fix/explain → follow up. 5) Silence breeds anxiety.
KC4: 1) Cost-plus floor, value ceiling. 2) Three = sweet spot; middle landing. 3) 50/50; 30/40/30 large jobs. 4) Added scope + price + days + confirm ask. 5) No-start-without-deposit.
KC5: 1) Date, client, service, amount, paid, balance. 2) Fixed costs ÷ margin per job. 3) After successful delivery, at follow-up. 4) Consent before public quotes/screenshots. 5) Ghosting a complaint.
KC6: 1) Who, offer, packages, acquisition, money, reputation. 2) Problem, approach, result. 3) Below floor you pay to work. 4) Named warm-circle five. 5) Pressure-testing, not judgement.

## 8. Expected outputs & calibration

S2: proposals timed; keep batch under 12 min. S4: pricing cards filed for A1 marking. S5: ledgers checked
for six fields. S6: P-gate witnessed; two assessors on failures.

## 9. Rubric (0–4)

0 cannot · 1 heavy prompts · 2 completes with gaps · 3 unaided, credible · 4 unaided, credible + teaches it back. Gate: sell-tomorrow plan.
