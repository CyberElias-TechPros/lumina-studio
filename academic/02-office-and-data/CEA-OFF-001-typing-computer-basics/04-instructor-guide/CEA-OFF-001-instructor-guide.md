**Do not distribute to students.** This guide contains expected answers, judgement guidance and
safety notes. Knowledge check answers below (Section 9) are intentionally concise — use them to
mark quickly and consistently, not to read out.

## 1. What this course is (and is not)

This is the academy's on-ramp. Success = a phone-confident beginner who leaves able to operate a
Windows machine independently, has an email account in use, and passed the unsupervised final
practical. It is **not** a typing certificate, not an IT support course, and not a place to teach
advanced topics. If a learner arrives already competent, accelerate them through Sessions 1–2 and
give them the workbook's stretch drills — do not slow the cohort for them, and do not let them sit
idle: pair them as peer mentors.

## 2. Preparation checklist (per session)

- Room: machines on and updated; monitor, keyboard, mouse present and clean; spare mouse; one
  machine per student where possible (pair-share only above 15 students).
- Display: projector or screen mirroring for demonstrations.
- Practice accounts: students logged in pre-session; passwords on cards (printed, not taped to
  monitors). One spare admin account.
- Files: practice USB drives ×2; `about-me.txt` pre-loaded on one; sample phishing emails printed
  (S4); typing drill cards printed (S2); workbook page per student.
- Environment: power strips safe, no trailing cables; chair height adjusted; a fan; water for long
  typing blocks.
- Your 10 minutes: re-read this guide's session section; open the demo files; test the demo once.

## 3. Delivery model (105 minutes)

| Time    | Block                   | Instructor behaviour                                                                |
| ------- | ----------------------- | ----------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| 0–10    | Welcome & review        | Attendance; 2-min recall from last session ("show me…"); today's outcomes on screen |
| 10–25   | Topic introduction      | The lesson note's Section 3 narrative, in your words                                |
| 25–45   | Demonstration           | Narrate every action; name keys/clicks aloud; check screens for faces               |
| 45–70   | Guided practice         | Circulate; correct finger placement and click style; unblock fast, explain once     |
| 70–95   | Independent practice    | Hands off; note who struggles; answer with questions, not by doing it for them      |
| 95–110  | Assessment & correction | Quizzes self-marked in room; error correction immediately, in plain words           |
| 110–120 | Summary & assignment    | Recap the "remember" lines; preview next session; stamp workbook                    |

## 4. Session notes and known failure points

**Session 1 — Understanding the Computer.** Machines differ; point at the actual unit in the room,
not a generic diagram. Failure points: students powering the monitor only; holding the power button;
panicked password attempts (3 strikes locks practice accounts — know the reset path); left-handed
mice. End check: every student signs out unaided before the break.

**Session 2 — Keyboard & Mouse.** The baseline WPM test is diagnostic, not punitive — say so.
Failure points: eyes-on-keyboard habit (use tent cards blocking the keyboard for clipped students);
Caps Lock toggles; tension injuries (wrist position, breaks every 15 min — non-negotiable);
double-click speed mismatch (fix in Settings, not with sarcasm). Target this session: home-row
anchoring is automatic for 80% of students by minute 70; those not there get a take-home drill plan.

**Session 3 — Files & Internet.** Failure points: students saving to Desktop instead of folders;
Recycle Bin misconceptions; USB eject skipped (demonstrate corruption once — unsaved file vanishing
is the best teacher in the room); downloads with mismatched extensions. Never let a student execute
a downloaded `.exe` in class — model deleting it instead. If the room's internet is down, teach
offline with local files and postpone search/download portions; note it for the make-up slot.

**Session 4 — Digital Independence.** Sequential dependencies: email must work before the
attachment relay; check MFA together before anyone leaves. Failure points: weak passwords
(enforce 12+ chars; suggest a passphrase pattern: three random words + numbers); MFA codes expired;
form submission anxiety — sit with one student through the full flow. **The final practical is
observed, unaided, without commentary from the instructor** — mark silently; corrections happen
after the grade, in feedback, not during.

## 5. Assessment and marking guidance

- **Knowledge checks (10%):** in-session, 5 questions, self-marked with you; collect scores on the
  marking sheet. Straight answers; no tricks.
- **Practical exercises (25%):** workbook completion; sign each completed activity box; spot-check
  quality (file present, named correctly, correct location) rather than counting boxes.
- **Assignments (15%):** A1 (power sequence), A2 (file tidy) delivered in session 3/4; A3 (email
  practice) in session 4. Late = half marks; missing = 0, no nagging beyond one reminder.
- **Final project (40%):** run the FP brief exactly as written. One observation, no prompts, no
  coaching. Permitted: waiting, and answering procedural questions the student initiates
  ("May I close this window?") with "your choice". Use the rubric bands strictly (Section 11).
- **Show and tell (10%):** immediately after the final; the student narrates what they did and why.
  Score on clarity and honesty, not eloquence.

Critical practical gate (mandatory): power on/off sequence, save-and-locate, email-with-attachment.
A student can have 60% overall but **not yet competent** if the gate fails — they book the
reassessment within two weeks. Say this out loud on day one.

## 6. Equity and access notes

- Under-18 learners: parent/guardian consent per academy policy; photography never without consent.
- Vision/hearing/motor accessibility: larger displays, high-contrast themes, keyboard-only mouse
  alternatives (Windows Ease of Access), seating near the board. Ask; do not assume.
- Language: instruct in the clearest professional English; vernacular is welcome for explanation but
  technical terms stay standard (a student must recognise "File Explorer", not a translated alias).
- Data costs: all exercises work offline except the browser and email blocks; if internet fails,
  use local alternatives and note it.

## 7. Classroom management

Small class, big machines, mixed abilities. Double-monitor the two extremes: the fast ones who
finish early (give drills or mentor roles) and the frozen ones (stand beside them, breathe, one
step at a time). Never type on a student's keyboard uninvited — hands hover, don't touch. Questions
are the curriculum; praise specificity ("good, you checked the extension") not speed.

## 8. Moderation

Record one final-practical observation per cohort on the moderation sheet (another instructor
watches). Compare marks; discuss discrepancies; adjust descriptors, never lower the bar. Keep the
completed sheets with the cohort file.

## 9. Knowledge-check answer keys (instructor use)

**KC1:** 1) Any three hardware + any peripheral (e.g. monitor, keyboard, mouse + printer). 2) Sleep keeps state in memory for fast resume; shut down closes everything and powers off fully. 3) Start menu, bottom-left (or Ctrl key); opens applications, settings, power and search. 4) Holding the power button (except emergencies / instructor instruction). 5) A window is an
application's work area; move / resize / minimise / maximise / close.

**KC2:** 1) Left click selects/activates; right click opens the context menu; double-click opens. 2) F and J; the `;` is on the right-hand home row, used by the right pinky. 3) Caps Lock toggles
capital letters; the small LED on the key indicates it. 4) Undo; reverting a mistaken deletion or
edit. 5) Accuracy = correct characters ÷ typed characters; wrong speed wastes time retyping.

**KC3:** 1) The full location address of a file, e.g. `D:\Portfolio\design\flyer.png`. 2) F2 = rename; Ctrl+Shift+N = new folder. 3) Bin holding deleted items for restore; Shift+Delete
and USB-drive deletion bypass it. 4) Windows caches writes; eject flushes them safely. 5) Unexpected `.exe`/`.zip`, mismatched name vs content, pressure language, unknown sender,
"free" claims.

**KC4:** 1) 12+ characters, mixed types, unique; e.g. three-word passphrase + digits + symbol. 2) A file sent with the email; save it via the download link after checking sender/extension. 3) Urgency, odd sender, mismatched link, credential request, spelling errors (any three). 4) The link goes where the attacker wants; you type credentials into a fake page. 5) Provider's datacentres; protects against device loss/theft and enables sharing by link.

## 10. Expected outputs

- Session 2 log: every student has a baseline WPM/accuracy figure written in their workbook.
- Session 3 check: each student's folder structure visible on screen at least once.
- Session 4: every student demonstrates the final practical (pass) before certificates are requested.
- Course file: attendance register, marking sheet, rubric sheets, moderation sheet, feedback forms.

## 11. Final-practical rubric (0–4 per band; see also 11-marking-rubrics/)

| Band | Behavioural descriptor                                                         |
| ---- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------ |
| 0    | Cannot or will not attempt without constant prompts                            |
| 1    | Attempts; needs prompts for most steps; unsafe handling                        |
| 2    | Completes with occasional prompts / recoverable errors                         |
| 3    | Completes alone; explains adequately; minor inefficiency                       |
| 4    | Completes alone, efficiently and safely; clear explanation; anticipates issues |

Critical-gate failure at any band = Not Yet Competent, regardless of score.
