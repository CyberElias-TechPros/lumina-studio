**1 — Learning outcomes**

By the end of this session you will be able to:

1. Create an email account and sign in securely.
2. Compose and send an email with a file attached; download an attachment you receive.
3. Complete a basic online form (name, address, selection, submit) accurately.
4. Join or observe an online meeting link safely; save files to cloud storage.
5. Spot a scam email and a weak password; explain why each fails.
6. Complete the final unaided practical (observed by the instructor).

**2 — Prerequisite knowledge**

Sessions 1–3 complete. You can manage files and folders, browse and search, and download safely.
Bring your Session 3 assignment evidence — we look at it first.

**3 — Introduction**

Email is the one account that unlocks everything else: job applications, bank alerts, school
portals, government services, business invoices. A candidate without email in 2026 is invisible to
modern hiring. Today you create yours, use it for real work, and then — the point of the whole
course — you will do a full set of ordinary tasks completely alone while your instructor watches.
No prompts. That is what "independence" means, and it is exactly the evidence this certificate
will stand on.

**4 — Key concepts**

- **Email account** — a personal mailbox: address (name@provider), password, inbox, sent, drafts.
  Choose a professional address: your name, not a nickname you would hide in a job interview.
- **Password** — minimum 12 characters, mixed case, digits, a symbol; never reused across sites;
  never shared; never typed on request. **MFA** — a second proof (code or app) at sign-in; enable
  it wherever offered.
- **Attachment** — a file sent with an email. Attach → choose file → send. Downloading an
  attachment = saving it to your machine (check the sender, then the extension).
- **Online form** — questions collected over the web: text fields, dropdowns, checkboxes (e.g. the
  CEA application form). Check required fields marked * before submitting.
- **Online meeting** — a video classroom link (Meet, Zoom, Teams). Join from the link; camera and
  mic can be toggled; be early; be audible.
- **Cloud storage** — your files kept online (Google Drive, OneDrive): syncs, survives a dead
  laptop, shareable by link. Know where your files actually live.
- **Scam awareness (phishing)** — fake messages creating urgency: "Your account will close in 24
  hours", fake bank forms, too-good offers. Red flags: urgency, links that hide the real address,
  requests for passwords, spelling errors, unknown senders. Verify by opening the official site
  yourself, never via the link.
- **Digital responsibility** — you are accountable for what leaves your account. Check the
  recipient. Check the attachment. Once sent, it is sent.

**5 — Instructor demonstration**

1. Create a fresh email account on a provider the academy uses (Gmail or Outlook), narrating each
   choice: name, address availability, strong password, phone/recovery email, MFA setup.
2. Send an email to a practice classmate with an attachment (`about-me.txt` from Session 3);
   open it; download the attachment; reply-all with "Received".
3. Open the sample online form (academy practice form or a harmless public form); fill it fully,
   including a dropdown and a checkbox; submit; show the confirmation page.
4. Join the class meeting link; mute/unmute; camera on/off; leave properly.
5. Save a file to cloud storage; find it again from a different location (or signed-out view).
6. Live scam demo: show three emails — one legitimate, two phishing — and have the room vote
   which is fake and why.

**6 — Guided practice**

1. **Email clinic (25 min):** create your account; send yourself one email with subject
   `Week-2 check-in` and the text "My first course email"; download and open the message.
   Fill in the strongest password you can remember unaided (workbook slot provided).
2. **Attachment relay (10 min):** partner A emails partner B `homework.txt`; partner B downloads it,
   renames it `homework-received.txt`, emails it back with a one-line reply.
3. **Form filling (10 min):** complete the practice form end-to-end without skipping required
   fields; correct any validation errors read one at a time.
4. **Safety drill (10 min):** in pairs, sort ten printed scenarios into "real" / "scam"; defend
   one choice aloud.

**7 — Independent practice**

Procedural run-through, alone, in preparation for the final: power on → login → create folder
`Final-Practice` → write a note in Notepad naming the folder → save it inside → find an official
document online → download it → compose an email to your practice partner with the document
attached → send → explain out loud what you would do if the mail bounced.

**8 — Real-world example**

An MTN agent on Agip Road once processed a "bank reactivation" email she received at home because
it looked exactly like her bank's letters — same colours, same footer. It cost the customer ₦85,000.
Every element of that fake email is something you can spot now: urgency, a mismatched sender
address, a link that did not match the bank's real domain, and a request for credentials no bank
ever asks for by email. You are the filter between your family and those messages.

**9 — Knowledge check 4**

1. What makes a password strong? Give an example pattern you can remember.
2. What is an attachment, and how do you download one safely?
3. Name three scam red flags.
4. Why should you never click a link in a "your account will close" email?
5. Where do files stored “in the cloud” physically live, and what does that protect you from?

**10 — Common mistakes and troubleshooting**

- **Weak or reused passwords:** the number-one reason accounts are stolen. Use 12+ characters;
  a password manager or a written card kept at home (never in the same bag as the laptop).
- **Sending to the wrong person:** check the address before Send; use a draft first for anything
  important.
- **Big attachments:** most providers reject files over ~25 MB; use a cloud link instead.
- **Forgetting MFA codes:** codes expire in minutes; if yours fails, request a new one — do not
  ask anyone else for theirs.
- **Panic clicks on scary emails:** breathe; look at the sender; if unsure, delete — banks never
  close accounts via email link.
- **Typing fast into forms:** required-field errors are normal; read each message, fix one by one.

**11 — Lesson summary**

- Email = your digital identity hub; choose a professional address and guard it.
- Password 12+ characters, unique; MFA on everywhere.
- Attachments: sender and extension before opening; cloud links for big files.
- Forms: required fields first, confirmation page last.
- Scam rule: urgency + link + credentials = not real. Verify on the official site yourself.
