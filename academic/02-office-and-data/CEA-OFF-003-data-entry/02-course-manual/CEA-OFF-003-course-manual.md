## What you will walk out with

A measured, verifiable skill set: your accuracy rate on two timed tests, a cleaned real-world
spreadsheet to take to interviews, a file-organisation system, and an honest one-page report of
your own numbers. Not a vague promise that you are "good at data entry" — evidence.

## The four sessions

### S1 — Data Discipline

What data entry really is: independent records, not typing. Fields (name, date, phone, amount),
data types (text, number, date, boolean), the accuracy mindset, attention to detail, forms vs
tables vs spreadsheets. Speed is meaningless below 98% accuracy; we build accuracy first, speed second.

### S2 — Spreadsheets for Data Work

Rows and columns as records and fields. Entering and formatting data — dates DD/MM/YYYY, phone
numbers stored as text with leading zeros, names in Title Case. Sorting, filtering, removing
duplicates, freeze panes. You will clean a 200-row messy sheet.

### S3 — Transcription & Validation

Copying accurately from source documents: forms, receipts, paper registers. Double-keying
spot-check technique. File naming conventions (`2026-11-member-register-v2.xlsx`), folder
organisation hierarchies, validation rules (required fields, formats, ranges), error checking
against source — flag, never guess.

### S4 — Accuracy & Speed Practical

The measured day: accuracy test and speed test on realistic data, a full spreadsheet cleanup, and
the one-page report — rows processed, errors found, accuracy %, speed, what you'd improve next.

## Assessment weighting

KCs 10% · workbook 25% · A1 cleaned register 15% · accuracy/speed final 40% · show-and-tell 10%.
Your accuracy final must reach 98% — that is the certificate gate. Speed is reported honestly, not gated.

## Classroom rules

Source stays visible and unmarked while typing. Yellow = flagged question, never a blank to fill
by guesswork. One sitting for each timed test. Phones away during timed drills.

## What to bring

Reading glasses if you use them; a stopwatch (or borrow one); a folder for your printed artefacts.
No internet required during class.

## Where to get help

Your instructor during sessions; CEA /blog free data-entry notes; Microsoft Support and Google
Sheets Help Center from home.
