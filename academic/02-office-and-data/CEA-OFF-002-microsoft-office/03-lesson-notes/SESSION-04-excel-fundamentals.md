**1 — Outcomes**
Explain workbook vs worksheet; navigate rows, columns, cells, ranges; understand cell references;
enter and edit data; format cells (number, date, currency, borders, widths, heights); write basic
formulas; use SUM, AVERAGE, MIN, MAX, COUNT.

**2 — Prerequisites**
Sessions 1–3 (documents side). Typing of numbers expected. No formulas background assumed.

**3 — Introduction**
Word tells the story; Excel does the arithmetic. The same expenses sheet that took Grace Stores 45
minutes by calculator with mistakes now takes 3 minutes with formulas that cannot be wrong. Every
shop, salon, school and NGO in Port Harcourt runs on spreadsheets — today you learn the grammar.

**4 — Key concepts**
Workbook (.xlsx, many sheets — tabs at bottom) vs worksheet; cell address (B7 = column B row 7);
ranges (B2:B20); active cell, Name Box, formula bar; data types (text, numbers, dates — Excel stores
dates as serials); number formats (General, Number 2dp, Currency ₦, Percentage 1dp, Date DD/MM/YYYY);
raw vs formatted (₦1,500 vs 1500 — same number); borders & fills for structure; column width/row
height (double-click border to autofit); formulas always start `=`; SUM(range), AVERAGE(range),
MIN(range), MAX(range), COUNT(range); cell references update when you drag the fill handle.

**5 — Demonstration**
Build a sales sheet live for Grace Stores: columns A date, B item, C qty, D unit price, E line total
(=C2*D2); SUM for column E; AVERAGE of qty; MAX/MIN of line totals; format E as Currency ₦; dates
as DD/MM/YY; autofit columns; border the table; freeze top row (View → Freeze). Save as
`sales-2026-11.xlsx`.

**6 — Guided practice**
Students replicate the sheet from a printed handout with 8 rows of data. Steps verified cell by
cell: type, format, formulas, autofit. Instructor circulates. Everyone ends with a working total.

**7 — Independent practice**
Build your own 10-row list (expenses, marks, inventory — your choice) with at least one SUM, one
AVERAGE and one MIN or MAX; format currency where money; save to `CEA-2026\Office\`.

**8 — Real-world example**
A catering business in Rumuola paid staff on "rough-rough" estimation until her niece built the
same sheet we built today; the first month it caught ₦18,000 of overpayment. A formula is a
politician who never lies.

**9 — Knowledge check 4**

1. Workbook vs worksheet? 2. Cell C7 means? 3. Which symbol starts a formula?
2. What does Currency format show that General doesn't? 5. Write the formula for the total of cells E2 to E11.

**10 — Common mistakes**
Forgetting the `=`; typing ₦ into a number and getting text (let the format add it); blaming Excel
for #VALUE! — it means the cell holds text where a number was expected; merged cells breaking
references — avoid merge, use Center Across Selection; deleting with Backspace before weak formulas
(always click the cell, read the formula bar).

**11 — Summary**
Workbook>sheet>cell>range. Formats make numbers readable — don't type symbols. Formulas start with
= and update when dragged. SUM/AVERAGE/MIN/MAX/COUNT cover 90% of beginner needs.

**12 — Assignment**
A2 begins: this session's sheet becomes your expense tracker — add one more week of data and a
chart next session; due end of S5. 7.5%.

**13 — Reflection**
Formulas click slowly for some; that is normal. By S5 you sort, filter and chart — by S6 you present.
