# CEA Lesson Note — Session 6: PowerPoint

**1 — Outcomes**
Create a presentation; manage slides and layouts; apply themes; add text, images, shapes and icons;
use transitions and animations sparingly; add speaker notes; present in slideshow mode; design
basic, good-looking slides; deliver aloud in front of the class.

**2 — Prerequisites**
Sessions 1–5 — especially tables, images and export.

**3 — Introduction**
A CV gets you read; a spreadsheet proves your skill; a presentation gets you heard. Ten minutes of
design sense decides whether the room leans in or out. Today: build a 6-slide deck and deliver it.

**4 — Key concepts**
Slides, layouts (Title, Title & Content, Section Header), themes & variants, master-ish consistency
(fonts, accent), text rules (max ~6 lines, ~30 words per slide, big enough type — ≥24pt body),
images (one strong image > five clipart relics; alt text), shapes & icons for diagrams, transitions
(fade, subtle) vs animations (rare, purposeful), speaker notes (your script, hidden in show mode),
F5 show from start / Shift+F5 from current, Esc to exit; deck flow: Hook → Context → 3 points →
Ask/Close. Rule of thirds-ish: title left or centred, generous margins, contrast.

**5 — Demonstration**
Build from blank: slide 1 title + your name; slide 2 "Why this matters" (context); slides 3–4 two
key points with one image each; slide 5 numbers from your expense sheet (screenshot or chart copy);
slide 6 close + thank-you + question invitation. Theme: pick one, restrict palette. Add speaker
notes to each slide; run F5; demonstrate Esc; export to PDF (print handout 2-up optional).

**6 — Guided practice**
Students draft their own deck for a fictional business pitch (Grace Stores wants to sell online
vs the owner wants staff training — pick a side, 5 slides max): title, problem, solution, numbers,
ask. Instructor checks each slide's word count and type size out loud.

**7 — Independent practice**
Finish the final-project deck: 5–7 slides on a real goal (your CV walk-through, a club proposal, a
business pitch). Rehearse aloud once; record nothing yet — delivery comes in assessment.

**8 — Real-world example**
A church in Rumuola lost its only silent projector to a "full of text" deck; the projector didn't
leave — it hid. The rewrite rule used everywhere: if a slide needs you reading it, it's a handout,
not a slide. Speak; let the slide support, never duplicate.

**9 — Knowledge check 6**

1. Max words/lines per slide (rule of thumb)? 2. Title slide contents? 3. Transition vs animation?
2. What do speaker notes do? 5. F5 vs Shift+F5?

**10 — Common mistakes**
Wall of text; animations on every element; unreadable contrast; clip-art collage; "thank you" as
the only closer; reading slides aloud verbatim; decks over 10 minutes nobody asked for.

**11 — Summary**
Six slides, one idea each, big type, one accent colour, real images, notes for you, F5 to present,
PDF to share. Your voice is the show — the deck is the backdrop.

**12 — Final practical**
The observed "Three artefacts" demonstration happens in this session's second half or at the
scheduled catch-up: CV/letter PDF, expense sheet with live SUM + chart (printed), and 5–7 slide
presentation delivered aloud. See CEA-ACA-FP-OFF-002 and rubric CEA-ACA-RUB-OFF-002.

**13 — Reflection**
You just built a complete professional toolkit. Data Entry (CEA-OFF-003) deepens the spreadsheet
side; Digital Marketing (CEA-BIZ-001) uses this deck to sell.
