## 10–25 · Teaching block

**Research with AI.** Use it to map a field quickly: "Explain the main schools of thought on X;
list their key proponents" — then treat every name/claim as unverified until a primary source says
so. Search-grounded modes reduce staleness but not error. For anything going to a client/manager:
claim → source url/page → initialled check.

**Long-document summarising.** Paste/feed text → "Summarise in 1 page for a busy manager; keep
caveats and numbers; flag anything unclear". Cross-check three extracted facts against the original.
Never cite the AI's summary as the source — cite the original.

**Drafting work.** Emails, memos, proposals, lesson plans, meeting minutes. AI removes blank-page
fear; you supply judgement: edit tone, strip hallucinated specifics, add local detail (names, dates,
prices — the model cannot know these; confirm them).

**The verification loop.** Every claim leaving your desk: (1) was it generated? (2) checked against
source? (3) source recorded beside it? (4) unchecked = removed or labelled "unverified". No exceptions
for "it's probably fine".

## Answers — KC3

1. It is not a source — it is an aggregation of probability, often unsourced. 2) Spot-check 3+ extracted
   facts; keep the original citation, not the summary. 3) Claim → source check → record → remove/label
   unverified. 4) Judgement, local facts, tone, accountability. 5) Never — cite the primary source; the
   summary is just notes.
