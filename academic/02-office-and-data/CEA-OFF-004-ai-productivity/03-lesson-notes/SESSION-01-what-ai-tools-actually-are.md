## 0–10 · Review & warm-up

Show a projected AI conversation that produced a very confident, completely wrong tax rule with a
plausible citation. Ask the room: "Would you have signed it?"

## 10–25 · Teaching block

**What an LLM is.** A statistical engine predicting the next token from context, trained on a huge
corpus up to a cut-off date. It does not look anything up, remember your company's prices, or know
today's news unless given it. Fluency ≠ truth.

**What they're good at:** drafting and rewriting, summarising, structuring ideas, explaining concepts
at your level, translation, tone-matching, first-pass analysis of text you give them, generating
examples and checklists.

**Where they fail:** invented facts (hallucinations) with confident citations; stale knowledge;
arithmetic slips; plausible-sounding legal/medical/financial specifics; bias inherited from training
data; no memory between sessions unless the tool keeps one — and even then, treat recall as
unverified.

**Choosing a tool.** Q/A chat models (ChatGPT, Claude, Gemini, Copilot) for text work; built-in ones
(Word, Sheets, Gmail, Canva) for in-app drafting; search-grounded modes for anything needing current
facts; code assistants for technical work. Free tiers are enough for this course. Never route private
client data through a free tool without the client's written say-so.

## 25–45 · Guided demonstration

Instructor live-prompts: "Summarise the plot of Hamlet in two lines" (model nails it) → "What was
the naira-to-dollar rate this morning?" (model hedges/invents) → "Give me three real court cases on
tenancy law in Nigeria with citations" (model produces one real, two invented citations) — class
flags which is which using the verification drill sheet.

## 45–70 · Guided practice

Pairs run the **failure-mode gallery**: five printed AI outputs (two correct, three hallucinated in
different ways — wrong number, invented source, outdated law). Teams label each claim VERIFIED /
SUSPECT / GUESS, then instructor reveals results. Debrief: fluency fooled everyone in at least one case.

## 70–95 · Independent practice

Solo: write **three questions you asked an AI last month** (from memory) and classify what you trusted
vs what you verified. Then run one of them live in-class and check the answer against a source.

## 95–110 · Correction & check

KC1: (1) Why does an LLM sound confident when wrong? (2) Name three tasks LLMs do well.
(3) Name three failure modes. (4) Cut-off date means what? (5) What single habit prevents
"confident nonsense" from reaching a client?
Self-mark; Q5 answer is "verification against a real source, every time".

## 110–120 · Summary & next session

We covered the machine; next we learn to steer it — prompts with role, context, constraints, examples.

## Answers — KC1

1. Training rewards fluent continuation, not truthfulness. 2) Any three: drafting, summarising,
   structuring, explaining, translating, examples. 3) Hallucination / stale knowledge / bias / maths
   slips (any 3). 4) No knowledge of events after training cut-off; may miss them unless search-grounded.
2. Verify every externally-leaving claim against a primary source.

## Homework (20 min)

Ask an AI to explain something you know cold (your trade, your hobby). Note three errors or gaps.
