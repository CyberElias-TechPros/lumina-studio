## 0–10 · Review & warm-up

Homework debrief: read three examples of the AI's errors about things students know cold.

## 10–25 · Teaching block

**The anatomy of a working prompt:** ROLE ("Act as a senior accountant…") · CONTEXT ("for a small
Port Harcourt pharmacy…") · TASK ("draft…") · CONSTRAINTS ("under 200 words; Nigerian English; no
legal advice") · EXAMPLE ("here is a sample I liked: …") · OUTPUT FORMAT ("table: Item | Issue |
Fix"). Context is the multiplier — most weak prompts miss it.

**Iteration, not perfection.** Round 1 gives a mediocre draft. Round 2: "Make the tone warmer; cut
jargon; add one concrete example" — refine, don't restart. Ask for critique ("what's weak in this
draft?"), for alternatives, for shorter/longer. Every professional relationship with AI is a
conversation, not a lottery ticket.

**Reusable prompt library.** Save prompts that worked, with the doc they suited and the checks
needed. Parameterise: `[CLIENT]`, `[TONE]`, `[LENGTH]`. Five strong prompts beat fifty mediocre ones.

## 25–45 · Guided demonstration

Live: (1) naive prompt "write a complaint letter" → bland. (2) add role+context+constraints → better.
(3) add an example + explicit format → near-usable. (4) iterate twice → usable. Project each stage
side-by-side; the room votes which version they would send.

## 45–70 · Guided practice

Pairs work on their OWN recurring task (chosen at briefing): draft v1 prompt, run it, critique,
iterate twice — logging each version and what changed. Instructor circulates with the context checklist.

## 70–95 · Independent practice

Solo: build your **Prompt Library card** — five prompts for five recurring tasks, each with: purpose,
the prompt, expected format, checks needed before use. Bring printed to S3/S4.

## 95–110 · Correction & check

KC2: (1) Two parts of a prompt that most change output quality. (2) What does "iterate, don't
restart" mean? (3) What is a reusable prompt library? (4) Output format constraint — example.
(5) Why include a bad-example? Self-mark.

## 110–120 · Summary & next session

Prompts are steering, not magic. Next: AI-assisted research and drafting — and the verification loop.

## Answers — KC2

1. Context & constraints (role and examples also strong). 2) Refine output through follow-ups
   rather than re-rolling the dice. 3) A saved set of tested prompts with formats/checks, parameterised
   for reuse. 4) "As a 3-column table", "under 150 words", "bulleted, max 6 points". 5) Models mirror
   the shape/tone of examples, including what you avoid.
