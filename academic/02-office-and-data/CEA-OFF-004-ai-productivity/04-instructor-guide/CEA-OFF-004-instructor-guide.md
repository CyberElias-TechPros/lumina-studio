**Do not distribute.** Keys in Section 7.

## 1. What this course is

AI literacy with integrity: students leave with a documented, verified, personal workflow. The class
lives or dies on verification discipline — if verification slides into rubber-stamping, stop the room
and redo a drill.

## 2. Preparation

Browser machines; approved accounts (free tiers fine); the hallucination corpus printout; failure-mode
gallery cards; verification-field sheets; A1 templates; stopwatch; offline fallbacks listed in case
service is degraded.

## 3. Delivery model

105 min: 0–10 hook · 10–25 teach · 25–45 demo · 45–70 guided · 70–95 independent/final · 95–110 KC ·
110–120 close. Demo prompts must be live — pre-recorded demos hide model variance.

## 4. Failure points

- S1: students quote a wrong AI answer back as "it's written so it must be true" — keep the
  confident-tax-rule hook ready.
- S2: endless prompt tweaking of a bad task — force them to define the task better first.
- S3: verification collapse (check 1, wave at 4 more) — the A1 rubric requires all five claims checked.
  No exceptions.
- S4: policy imitation ("copy yours and I'll sign") — every policy must reference the student's own
  recurring task; reject copies.

## 5. Assessment guidance

KCs self-marked; A1 marked on verification log (claims checked vs source quality), not style.
Final (40%): the one-page workflow + policy; gate = verification log completeness for the final.
Show-and-tell asks "show me a check that caught something" — reasoning judged, never eloquence.

## 6. Equity & access

Free-tier tools only; no paid features required. Low-vision students: high-contrast prompt panes.
None of the assessed artefacts requires a paid subscription or a personal AI account.

## 7. Knowledge-check keys

KC1: 1) Fluency rewarded, not truth. 2) Any 3: draft/summarise/structure/explain/translate/examples. 3) Hallucination / stale / bias / maths slips. 4) No knowledge past cut-off; needs search grounding or
material supplied. 5) Verify every externally-leaving claim against a source.
KC2: 1) Context & constraints. 2) Follow-up refinement, not re-rolling. 3) Saved/parameterised tested
prompts with formats & checks. 4) e.g. table/150 words/max 6 bullets. 5) Models mirror shape — be it kept good ones or bad ones.
KC3: 1) Not a source — aggregated probability without provenance. 2) Spot-check ≥3 facts; cite the
original, never the summary. 3) Claim → source → record → drop/label unverified. 4) Judgement, local
facts, accountability. 5) Never — cite primary source.
KC4: 1) Unverifiable stats / unstable citations / universal claims. 2) Private/client/credential data. 3) Submitted as unaided where independence is required, or policy-violating. 4) Tools allowed · data
never shared · verification · disclosure · learning stance. 5) AI accelerates; you verify.

## 8. Expected outputs & calibration

S2 end: everyone has ≥2 library prompts tested. S3 end: A1 covers real task. S4: nobody hands in a
policy with zero personal examples. First pilot: calibrate KC marking between instructors; adjust
descriptors at the same sitting.

## 9. Rubric (0–4)

0 cannot · 1 heavy prompts · 2 completes, shallow verify · 3 unaided, verified · 4 unaided, verified

- catches own error. Gate: verification log completeness, absolute.
