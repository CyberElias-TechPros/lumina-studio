## 0–10 · Review & warm-up

The interrupt-count experiment shared around; the humblest silence story wins the room.

## 10–25 · Teaching block

**Talking to humans under stress.** Drop tools, face the person, hands visible, no laptop barrier; speak slower than they do;
mirror their words ("so the POS freezes when you press End-of-Day…"); never say "obviously", "just", "anyone would know", "it's not rocket science" — every one of those sentences costs trust.

**Remote support with consent.** Ask first ("may I see your screen for 3 minutes?") · state every click before you do it ("now I'm opening…") · their cursor, not yours, when possible · never type their password — they type it · sign off screen by screen; tell them when you're finished and what changed.

**Password & account resets.** Verify identity by a known channel FIRST (never by asking "what's your password"); temporary passwords expire at first login; force change; log the reset; never send credentials in plain chat the client keeps forever — one-time tokens or verbal over call.

**Patience as method.** The 3-breath rule before any reply; the customer is never the enemy — the system failed them; a calm voice literally lowers their blood pressure; you are the adult in the room, always.

## 25–45 · Guided demonstration

Instructor role-plays "I hate computers" caller with volunteer — first take fast/jargon-heavy (watch faces) · second take paced/quiet/mirrored; debrief exact language swaps.

## 45–70 · Guided role-play

Pairs run four cards: (A) terrified novice, (B) angry power-user ("you people don't know anything"), (C) deaf-computer-user (customer says nothing works but machine is off), (D) leaver with no notice asking for old account password. Two minutes each + feedback phrases swapped.

## 70–95 · Independent practice

Solo: write your **reset playbook** — 5-step password-reset flow + two scripts (the calm open, the graceful close). File to workbook.

## 95–110 · Correction & check

KC3: (1) First 10 seconds script. (2) Remote-consent rule. (3) Temp-password lifecycle. (4) Two forbidden phrases. (5) 3-breath rule rationale.

## 110–120 · Summary & next session

Helping people is the job; machines are just the terrain. Next: the breadth — printers, passwords, patches.

## Answers — KC3

1. Name, time claim, mirror their problem ("I hear X — let's look together"). 2) Verbal consent first; narrate clicks; they type secrets; disconnect visibly. 3) One-time use, forced change at first login, logged, never emailed standing. 4) Any two listed ("just", "obviously", "rocket science"). 5) Your first instinct under pressure is wrong often enough to train a pause.
