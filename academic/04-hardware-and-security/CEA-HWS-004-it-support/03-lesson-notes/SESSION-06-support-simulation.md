## 0–10 · Review & warm-up

Room reset like a call center: headsets (or phones) on, boards ready, timer visible. Deep breath — responder mind.

## 10–25 · Teaching block (briefing)

**Format.** Eight seeded calls (prank-free, realistic): printer queue, Wi-Fi roaming, password reset, slow PC, permission denied, unknown-account lockout, "is this email real?", one out-of-your-depth (ransomware seed → correct move: isolate + L2 + don't promise). One volunteer role-plays while class listens live; observer cards mark steps and tone.

**Scoring.** Steps of the ladder hit · empathy language used · consent before remote · correct escalation judgment (the money round) · closing verification.

**The playbook reveal.** At the end, your A1 KB set gets clipped into a five-sheet "Support Playbook" to keep forever — you write it, you own it.

## 25–75 · Live simulations (observed)

Eight rounds: pair rotates responder (6 min) ↔ observer with card → debrief 1 min → rotate. Assessor logs P1–P6 per responder.

## 75–100 · Playbook compilation & show-and-tell

Solo: staple your playbook, one cover line ("Written by X, trainee support tech, 2026"). Two volunteers share the hardest call and what they noticed about their own voice.

## 100–110 · Correction & check

KC6: (1) Ladder four steps. (2) Difficult-caller first move. (3) Escalation package. (4) Close definition. (5) One phrase banned on this floor.

## 110–120 · Course close

Certificate gate: ≥2 faults diagnosed, ≥1 correctly escalated. Portfolio: playbook, A1, tickets, SLA card. Alumni: cybersecurity (SIEM/SOC direction), networking deep-dive, or go real — get a helpdesk job within 60 days.

## Answers — KC6

1. Reproduce, isolate, fix, confirm. 2) Lower your voice, mirror the words, make a plan aloud together. 3) Verbatim symptom, steps tried with results, logs attached. 4) User performs the task once successfully at their seat. 5) "Just" (or "obviously").

## Homework

None. Call in ready on your first real job — you will hear your own voice echoing these calls for years.
