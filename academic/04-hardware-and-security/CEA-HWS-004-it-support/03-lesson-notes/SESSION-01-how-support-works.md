## 0–10 · Review & warm-up

Ask: "Best IT support you ever got — why?" Answers: fast callback, plain words, actually fixed, followed up. All four are skills today.

## 10–25 · Teaching block

**The ticket lifecycle.** Report arrives (call/chat/email/walk-up) → acknowledge within SLA → triage (what broke, who's blocked, since when) → priority (impact × urgency) → assign → work → verify with user → close with notes. Tickets are the fossil record; memory is not
available to the next tech.

**Priority matrix.** P1 — business stopped (till down, server dead): minutes · P2 — one person fully blocked: same-day · P3 — workaround exists: 1–3 days · P4 — cosmetic/request: next service window. Never let P3 drown P1 — say no politely with the queue as witness.

**SLA thinking.** Promise what you can keep ("acknowledged in 15, diagnosis in 60, or I call you with a plan"); under-promise, over-deliver; the callback IS the SLA most people remember.

**Channels & tone.** Phone: smile audibly, slow down, no hold > 2 min without updates · text/chat: short, numbered steps · email: subject = asset + symptom ("Laptop-03 — can't open PDFs") · walk-up: drop tools, eyes on THEM first, machine second.

## 25–45 · Guided demonstration

Projected live queue: 8 tickets arrive in 60 seconds on the board; instructor triage-aloud assigns P-numbers, explains queue order, writes the acknowledgement texts; class challenges two assignments — debate; consensus reached.

## 45–70 · Guided practice

Pairs run **triage storm** — 20 tickets, 6 minutes: assign P1–P4 + one-line next move each; compare; dispute the three messiest (printer for CEO vs payroll deadline vs "mouse feels funny").

## 70–95 · Independent practice

Solo: your **personal SLA card** — your promises (ack, diagnose, update cadence, escalation triggers) in plain words, pocket-size. File to workbook.
