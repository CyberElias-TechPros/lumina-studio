## 0–10 · Review & warm-up

Two volunteers narrate their startup-kill wins; room confirms or rescues.

## 10–25 · Teaching block (briefing)

**The intake-to-return workflow.** (1) Intake: symptom in customer's words, back-up job card, photos of machine as received,
signature for data acknowledged. (2) Diagnose: ladder (power → display → heat → POST → boot → OS), fault card-driven.
(3) Fix + verify twice. (4) Service: dust, paste check, updates, backup proof. (5) Handover: explain what was wrong in plain words,
receipt with work done + warranty line ("3 months on labour") + care advice; never oversell; always honest "the HDD is dying — back it up today".

**Final practical format.** Deliberately faulted machine, one hour, solo: intake notes → diagnosis (ladder) → fix → service →
handover sheet. Verifier runs POST + two tests + one user story aloud.

## 25–90 · Final practical (observed)

Solo rotation through faulted units (PSU ajar / RAM out / heat-crusted / dead-CMOS / unbootable bcd) — one per student; assessor observes, never coaches mid-fault; time logged.

## 90–110 · Debrief & show-and-tell

Fault stories in 2 minutes each (symptom → hypothesis → fix → proof) — class votes "would you trust this tech?"; certificates gate announcement; feedback forms; next steps (A+ pathway, cybersecurity for network-savvy repairs).

## 100–110 · Correction & check

KC8: (1) Five phases of intake-to-return. (2) Job card must include what about data. (3) Ladder first three wrongs. (4) Warranty line typical. (5) Handover language rule.

## 110–120 · Course close

Certificate gate: safety witnessed throughout + machine returned working or honestly triaged. Portfolio: fault stories, service template, A1 essays.

## Answers — KC8

1. Intake → diagnose → fix+verify → service → handover. 2) Signed acknowledgement of data risk + backup taken Y/N. 3) Power/display/heat — 80% of bench heroics live here. 4) "3 months labour warranty" is the local norm; parts per vendor terms. 5) Plain words about what failed, what we did, what to watch — no jargon, no oversell.
