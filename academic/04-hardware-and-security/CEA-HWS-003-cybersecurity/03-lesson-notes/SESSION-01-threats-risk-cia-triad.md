## 0–10 · Review & warm-up

Ask: "Whose phone got hacked or almost did?" — hands will rise. "Today we learn to say WHY that happened in professional words."

## 10–25 · Teaching block

**CIA triad.** Confidentiality (only the right people see it — encryption, access) · Integrity (it wasn't changed — hashes, approvals, backups) ·
Availability (it's there when needed — redundancy, backups, DDoS awareness). Every control maps to one leg; imbalance = hole.

**Vocabulary the auditor uses.** Asset (what we protect) · Threat (who/what can hurt it) · Vulnerability (the hole — unpatched app, weak password) ·
Risk = likelihood × impact · Control (guard — MFA, policy, backup) · Residual risk (what's left; accepted in writing by whoever outranks you).

**Threat actors.** Criminals (fraud, ransom), insiders (careless or malicious), nation-state (rare for you, real for banks), accidents (you, 6pm on a Friday).
Most Nigerian losses: social engineering + reused passwords + missing MFA.

**Risk-ranked priorities.** Fix the likely-and-costly first: email account takeover, WhatsApp impersonation, mobile money SIM swap, shared shop password.

## 25–45 · Guided demonstration

Project a small business ("Grace Stores' fictional officer Adaeze") — assets: POS till, customer list, WhatsApp line, bank app; threat table filled live with class input; risk = likelihood × impact grid plotted on whiteboard.

## 45–70 · Guided practice

Pairs: same exercise for "a church treasurer" / "a tailor's WhatsApp catalogue" / "a clinic's patient register" — assets, two threats each, one control each. Swap and challenge.

## 70–95 · Independent practice

Solo: your **personal asset map** — 10 digital assets you own (email, bank app, SIM, cloud photos, laptop) + one likely threat each + one control you'll add this week. File to workbook.

## 95–110 · Correction & check

KC1: (1) CIA stands for? (2) Risk = ___ × ___. (3) Asset vs threat example. (4) Two Nigerian-daily threat patterns. (5) Residual risk owner.

## 110–120 · Summary & next session

Words first, then shields. Next: who gets the keys — authentication and awareness.

## Answers — KC1

Confidentiality, Integrity, Availability. 2) Likelihood × impact. 3) Asset = the thing (email, till, list); threat = what can hurt it (phishing, theft, crash). 4) Social engineering + password reuse (any 2 incl.
