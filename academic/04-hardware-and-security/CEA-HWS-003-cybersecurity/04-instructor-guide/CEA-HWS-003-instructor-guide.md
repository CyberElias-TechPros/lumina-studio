**Do not distribute.** Keys in Section 7.

## 1. What this course is

Behaviour change disguised as an IT course. The final report matters because the habits are the point;
assess risk judgment, never trivia. Shame-free classroom — most of you sending "kindly resend BVN" almost fell for one too.

## 2. Preparation

Curated safe-example vault (scroll-screenshots of real scams, redacted) · router screenshot pack ·
printable 40-card sort · NIST-ish response flow poster · backup demo drive · assignment cards for fictional orgs.

## 3. Delivery model

105 min: 0–10 story hook · 10–25 teach · 25–45 demo · 45–70 guided drill · 70–95 solo · 95–110 KC · 110–120 close.

## 4. Failure points

- S4: students read scams aloud laughing, then click experimentally — mouse-hand demo never; the gallery is screen-shown.
- S5: ISP-router-locked homes deflate good intentions — acknowledge the constraint; password rot + DNS change still wins big.
- S7: "pull the plug" panic-power — correct: disconnect network, preserve machine for forensics when possible; power-pull is last.
- S8 gate: findings without owners/dates fail; do not let "top 5 fears" substitute for an assessment.

## 5. Assessment guidance

A1: audit completeness + photos as evidence. Final: P1–P6 with absolute gate P4 (every finding owner + date + rank).
Show-and-tell: board-pitch reasoning.

## 6. Equity & access

Low-data: all examples printed; WhatsApp-only learners fully supported; no personal accounts inspected; anonymised everything.

## 7. Knowledge-check keys

KC1: 1) Confidentiality–Integrity–Availability. 2) Likelihood × impact. 3) Asset = thing; threat = what hurts it. 4) Phishing/reuse (any 2). 5) The owner in writing.
KC2: 1) Who vs what you may do. 2) Independent layers must all fall. 3) Standard users; admin for installs only. 4) Any two urgency frames. 5) Call known number; never the message's contact.
KC3: 1) 4–5 random words + number. 2) Reuse + weak passwords. 3) App > SMS > none. 4) Silent forwarding exfiltrates resets. 5) App-MFA + email 2FA + re-pinned bank app.
KC4: 1) True destination domain. 2) No one ever needs it. 3) Any two listed patterns. 4) `invoice.pdf.exe` / `.html` after image. 5) Call known · app self-open · trusted reader.
KC5: 1) Admin password. 2) WPA3 > WPA2 + strong PSK > open. 3) No LAN visibility for guests. 4) Fake SSID clone. 5) Programs open ports unasked.
KC6: 1) Private pipe + identity — NOT honesty. 2) Locked padlock on impostor. 3) Same-key vs public/private pair. 4) Self-XSS — never. 5) Not e2e — treat as readable.
KC7: 1) Stop spread first. 2) 3 copies / 2 media / 1 offsite. 3) Lottery ticket. 4) TELCO first, then bank. 5) Timeline, cause, fix, sign-off.
KC8: 1) Acceptable-use / password-MFA / incident duty. 2) Assets → threats → weaknesses → recommendations → checklist. 3) Rank + owner + date. 4) Boss-accepted remainder. 5) Evidence per line at day 30.

## 8. Expected outputs & calibration

S4: sort drills scored on the board every cohort. S7: tabletops timed; slow first responder normal. S8: two-assessor gate; board Q&A must include one "what if it's the POS till?".

## 9. Rubric (0–4)

0 dangerous · 1 heavy prompts · 2 recognises with hints · 3 acts unaided correctly · 4 unaided + trains others. Gate: actionable findings.
