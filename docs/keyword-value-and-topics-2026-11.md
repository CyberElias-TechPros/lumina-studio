# What your keywords are worth internationally, and what to write next

Assembled 2026-11-25 from the six-month Search Console export, published market
research, and a direct visual check of the image library.

## First, the honest limit

What is used instead is secondary but real: research-house market forecasts,

## The finding, in one paragraph

Your site earns its impressions from **low-value queries** and its rankings from
**high-value ones** — and the effort has gone the wrong way. The largest cluster
in the export, KB/MB/GB conversion, is 82 queries and 276 impressions and is
worth almost nothing internationally, because Google answers the question above
the results. The security terms — Zero Trust, vulnerability assessment, the SOC —
rank in your **top five positions** and sit inside markets growing at
double-digit rates with millions of unfilled roles worldwide. Those are the terms
that travel, and they are where 63.7% of your impressions but only 13% of your
clicks are coming from.

## The best topics to write on — ranked

Ranked by *value of the keyword × whether you can honestly answer it × whether a
page already exists*. That is why the top entry is not the biggest keyword.

**1. How to get into cybersecurity without a degree** — ✅ written this release.
Highest-intent unwritten query you had, and the one you are structurally able to
answer better than a US content farm, because you have a real classroom and real
learners.

**2. How to write a vulnerability assessment finding an engineer will act on** —
next. You rank **2.04** for the parent term. This is the sub-topic employers
actually test in interviews, and it is the gap between "I know the definition" and
"I can do the job". Your existing tool produces exactly this artefact, so the
article and the tool reinforce each other.

**3. SOC analyst: what the first 90 days actually look like** — the career-shaped
version of a term you already rank for. Do not write a second "what is a SOC"
page; `/blog/the-soc-room` owns that and cannibalising it was already a mistake
once.

**4. CVSS scoring without the maths anxiety** — the highest-volume technical
sub-question under your best-ranking term, and the one the assessment tool already
half-teaches.

**5. Digital signature vs electronic signature — the difference that is legal,
not technical** — small keyword, unusually high commercial intent, because the
searcher is usually a business deciding what to buy.

**6. Micro-segmentation, explained with a building analogy** — the top Zero Trust
branch you have not covered. Keep the analogy honest: say "analogy" in the copy.

**Not on this list, deliberately:** anything else in the KB/MB/GB cluster. You
have 276 impressions and zero clicks there because the answer is above the
results, and no page you write changes that. The existing page is the ceiling.

## Projected reader value, not traffic promises

No traffic forecast is given here, because a forecast built on unretrieved Trends
data would be a guess wearing a number's clothing. What can be said precisely:
Nigerian traffic converts **2.4×** better than the site average (3.50% against
1.47%) while carrying 36% of impressions, and
the international impressions you already have are 63.7% of the total at a 0.31%
CTR. Closing that international gap on the security terms is the single largest
measurable opportunity in the export — and it needs no new keyword to go after,
only better titles, better openings, and pages that answer the question in the
first sentence.
