# Afkit dropshipping: assessment and safe implementation path

**Status:** exploratory. The user says Afkit allows dropshipping and has granted permission to use its product photos. The available message does not establish wholesale pricing, permission for automated collection, stock reservations, delivery terms, a supplier order API or payout details. A gadget inquiry catalogue is implemented on this branch but has not been deployed. No Merchant Center account has been changed, and no physical-goods payment or supplier order flow has been enabled by this work. Do not use the digital-product shop's checkout for physical devices: it does not model addresses, inventory, supplier costs, delivery, warranties or physical-order fulfillment.

## What we observed

The public Afkit catalogue at `https://afkit.ng/shop/listing` showed 88 products at the time of this investigation, with paginated listings for laptops, phones, monitors and accessories. The count is a snapshot, not a verified complete or current supplier inventory. Many listed devices had only one or a few units and some showed two prices, missing visible quantities, truncated names or specific defects. Several are UK-used. These details must not be guessed or lost. A daily import alone cannot prevent sales of a unit that sells elsewhere between updates. Afkit's advertised warranty, delivery and payment-on-delivery terms for its own customers must not be promised by CEA until Afkit confirms they apply to CEA dropship orders.

## Recommended business model

Start with a small, manually reviewed pilot and CEA as the retailer of record: CEA displays the real total, collects the customer's payment using a verified payment flow, is responsible for the customer's support, and sends an accepted order to Afkit only after the exact device, current supplier cost, stock and delivery terms are confirmed. Negotiate a wholesale/partner price; if CEA buys at Afkit's retail price and adds fees and a sustainable margin, CEA may be more expensive than Afkit. Compare actual competitor pricing before listing; a margin cannot be guaranteed without the actual negotiated costs and liabilities.

Before accepting funds, **ideally** obtain a time-limited supplier reservation with an agreed price. If the supplier cannot reserve stock, make that uncertainty clear and use a documented confirmation/refund workflow; do not present unconfirmed inventory as guaranteed. The operational sequence should be:

1. Supplier feed or permitted read-only snapshot → validate stable SKU/variant, exact condition and defects, permitted images, stock, timestamp and confirmed cost → review price and selling terms.
2. Buyer selects item and delivery option → server rechecks current price and availability; hold/reserve stock if supported → record immutable order snapshot and idempotency reference.
3. Customer pays through hosted checkout → verify server-to-server, matching provider reference, amount, currency, status and order; handle duplicate events and ambiguous outcomes. A browser redirect is not proof of payment.
4. Once payment and supplier acceptance are confirmed, send minimum necessary fulfillment information over an agreed secure channel → record supplier acknowledgement, cost, tracking and exceptions. If the supplier rejects or price/stock changed, do not silently substitute or charge more; contact the customer and provide the agreed refund or alternative.
5. Reconcile payment, fees, refund/chargeback exposure and supplier invoice. Initially use reviewed payouts rather than automatically transferring money. Resolve delivery, damaged/misdescribed items, returns and warranty claims under agreed written terms.

The API supports Paystack subaccounts/split payments, but this requires Afkit's cooperation, verified recipient details, agreed fee allocation and a tested refund/chargeback arrangement. A split at payment time may pay the supplier before stock acceptance and complicate refunds; it is not automatically the best choice. Alternatively, CEA can settle verified supplier invoices after confirmation; this needs working capital because payment success and bank settlement are separate events. Choose only after supplier and payment-provider confirmation. Never store bank credentials or collect card details in the app.

## What is implemented locally

- **Shop → Browse gadgets** links to `/shop/gadgets`, a manually transcribed public-catalogue snapshot with Afkit's own image URLs (the user confirmed photo permission). It has 87 distinct observed items, while Afkit reported 88; this discrepancy means the export is not proven complete. Items are inquiries only: the CEA WhatsApp link identifies the exact observed title and photo, and price, condition and availability must be checked before taking an order. The snapshot is static and needs manual replacement; it is not daily synchronization, live inventory, checkout or a Merchant Center feed. Images remain hosted by Afkit and may disappear or change. Product IDs have not been supplied and image URLs are references, not stable SKUs. Do not promise product availability, delivery or pricing based on this snapshot.
- **Admin → Supplier review** is a browser-only workspace. Paste a markdown-rendered listing snapshot or the output of the optional collector. It keeps the original observed title including visible defects, marks missing stock as unknown, flags multiple prices rather than selecting one, and estimates prices under editable assumptions. It does not save, sync, publish, accept orders, contact Afkit or transfer money. Reloading clears the review. The existing live-mode app shell restricts this screen to the admin role in the browser; it is not server-side access control for stored data. Mock/demo mode intentionally bypasses that check, so do not enter confidential data on a shared or untrusted device or in a demo.
- An **experimental, manually invoked** browser collector, `node scripts/afkit-review-snapshot.mjs --permission-confirmed`, visits the public paginated listing and writes a review-only markdown snapshot under the git-ignored `supplier-reviews.local/` directory. It is intentionally **not scheduled** or connected to the storefront. Obtain explicit supplier authorization for automated access and reuse, and inspect applicable terms before using that flag. Install this project's dependencies and the Playwright Chromium browser (`npx playwright install chromium`) in an approved environment. It uses an isolated browser without signed-in credentials, limits pages and requests to Afkit hosts, and fails without writing a snapshot if counts, page reads or duplicate-image checks look inconsistent. A matching count does not prove completeness, stable identity or current stock. The collector has only been tested against synthetic HTML, not the live site; the development environment could not make direct network connections to Afkit. It may need adjustment with supplier-approved documentation. Avoid repeatedly hammering the supplier.
- Pricing defaults are **illustrations only**, editable in the UI: 1.5% plus ₦100 estimated fee (conservatively ignoring the local fee cap/waiver), 3% reserve, 8% target net margin, ₦5,000 minimum estimated profit and rounding up to ₦100. The operator must enter other per-item costs or explicitly confirm zero before estimates appear. The tool prices using the larger of the target-margin and minimum-profit thresholds, after estimated costs, fee and reserve, with integer kobo and conservative rounding. It cannot guarantee profitability. International card fees, actual supplier cost, delivery, payment-fee tax, returns, tax obligations, disputes and other losses may differ. Paystack's [published Nigeria pricing](https://paystack.com/pricing) should be checked against CEA's actual contract and payment channels.
- An optional regular-price comparison shows the maximum **estimated** reduction above that floor. Nothing automatically sends an offer or changes checkout. Never invent a higher original price, fake a sale, or use pressure or covert discounts. Contacting an abandoning shopper requires a lawful basis, transparent notice, an easy opt-out and Nigerian legal review; checkout details are not blanket marketing consent.

## Afkit questions to settle in writing

> Thanks again for agreeing to dropshipping. Could you share your partner/wholesale price list and stable SKU or variant IDs? Do you have an API or regularly updated CSV/JSON feed for prices, exact stock, product condition/defects, images and delivery costs, and may we automatically access it and reuse the descriptions on cea.ng and the product data and images on Google Merchant Center? Can you reserve a specific item and price while a customer checks out, and for how long? How should we submit paid orders securely, receive acceptance/rejection and tracking, and handle changed prices, cancellations, refunds, chargebacks, warranty claims, damaged products and out-of-stock orders? Which areas and delivery charges apply to our customers? Do you support a Paystack subaccount/split or do you prefer reconciled supplier invoices? Who is the support contact and what are your fulfillment timelines?

Also agree who is the retailer of record, when and how the supplier is paid, whether packages carry CEA branding, customer-data processing/security and retention, test orders, service levels, audit and dispute handling. Do not ask Afkit to send secret credentials through public chat; arrange a secure channel.

## Google Merchant Center and next code work

CEA's existing `/shop` and generated Merchant feed are for a small static **digital** catalogue; they cannot safely represent volatile used hardware. Do not append draft observations to that feed. To launch physical products, first implement a separate authoritative database and stable product/variant mapping, supplier sync health and fail-closed freshness, reviewed content rights, condition/defect details, physical product pages, checkout, stock reservation/rechecks, shipping, refunds, order/fulfillment lifecycle and reconciliation. The product feed must derive from the same authoritative price, availability, condition and variant shown on crawlable landing pages and at checkout. Configure shipping and returns truthfully; do not invent GTINs, warranties or strikethrough prices. See [Google's product data specification](https://support.google.com/merchants/answer/7052112?hl=en) and [landing page requirements](https://support.google.com/merchants/answer/4752265?hl=en). Nigeria appears in Google's [beta target-country list](https://support.google.com/merchants/answer/7101265?hl=en-GB); availability and eligibility should be checked in the actual Merchant Center account.

After Afkit provides integration details: implement an authenticated, bounded sync with per-run audit, freshness expiry and alerts; stable IDs and variant-specific stock; an admin approval/pause queue; a server-calculated quote and idempotent physical checkout; provider-verified payments and inventory/price checks; supplier order submission with idempotency, acknowledgement and retries; explicit cancellation/refund and manual exception queues; tracking notifications and privacy controls; reviewed payout and ledger reconciliation; and dynamic feed/landing-page parity tests. Run contract tests with the supplier's sandbox or explicit authorization, concurrent stock/price-change tests, webhook replay/failure tests, and real staged fulfillment before any public sales. This requires more than adding keys.
