# Afkit dropshipping: assessment and safe implementation path

**Status:** exploratory. The user says Afkit allows dropshipping and has granted permission to use its product photos and automatically access the site every 12 hours. The available message does not establish wholesale pricing, stock reservations, delivery terms, a supplier order API or payout details. The gadget inquiry catalogue, refresh infrastructure, 18% pricing default and local offer calculator are deployed; existing Worker configuration may override the default. The first manual refresh succeeded, but the next scheduled run still needs verification. No Merchant Center account has been changed, and no physical-goods payment or supplier order flow has been enabled by this work. Do not use the digital-product shop's checkout for physical devices: it does not model addresses, inventory, supplier costs, delivery, warranties or physical-order fulfillment.

## What we observed

The public Afkit catalogue at `https://afkit.ng/shop/listing` showed 88 products at the time of this investigation, with paginated listings for laptops, phones, monitors and accessories. The count is a snapshot, not a verified complete or current supplier inventory. Many listed devices had only one or a few units and some showed two prices, missing visible quantities, truncated names or specific defects. Several are UK-used. These details must not be guessed or lost. A daily import alone cannot prevent sales of a unit that sells elsewhere between updates. Afkit's advertised warranty, delivery and payment-on-delivery terms for its own customers must not be promised by CEA until Afkit confirms they apply to CEA dropship orders.

## Recommended business model

Start with a small, manually reviewed pilot and CEA as the retailer of record: CEA displays the real total, collects the customer's payment using a verified payment flow, is responsible for the customer's support, and sends an accepted order to Afkit only after the exact device, current supplier cost, stock and delivery terms are confirmed. Negotiate a wholesale/partner price; if CEA buys at Afkit's retail price and adds fees and a sustainable margin, CEA may be more expensive than Afkit. Compare actual competitor pricing before listing; a margin cannot be guaranteed without the actual negotiated costs and liabilities.

Before accepting funds, **ideally** obtain a time-limited supplier reservation with an agreed price. If the supplier cannot reserve stock, make that uncertainty clear and use a documented confirmation/refund workflow; do not present unconfirmed inventory as guaranteed. The operational sequence should be:

1. Supplier feed or permitted read-only snapshot → validate stable SKU/variant, exact condition and defects, permitted images, stock, timestamp and confirmed cost → review price and selling terms.
2. Buyer selects item and delivery option → server rechecks current price and availability; hold/reserve stock if supported → record immutable order snapshot and idempotency reference.
3. Customer pays through hosted checkout → verify server-to-server, matching provider reference, amount, currency, status and order; handle duplicate events and ambiguous outcomes. A browser redirect is not proof of payment.
4. Once payment and supplier acceptance are confirmed, send minimum necessary fulfillment information over an agreed secure channel → record supplier acknowledgement, cost, tracking and exceptions. If the supplier rejects or price/stock changed, do not silently substitute or charge more; contact the customer and provide the agreed refund or alternative.
5. Reconcile payment, fees, refund/chargeback exposure and supplier invoice. Initially use reviewed payouts rather than automatically transferring money. Resolve delivery, damaged/misdescribed items, returns and warranty claims under agreed written terms.

The API supports Paystack subaccounts/split payments, but this requires Afkit's cooperation, verified recipient details, agreed fee allocation and a tested refund/chargeback arrangement. A split at payment time may pay the supplier before stock acceptance and complicate refunds; it is not automatically the best choice. Alternatively, CEA can settle verified supplier invoices after confirmation; this needs working capital because payment success and bank settlement are separate events. Choose only after supplier and payment-provider confirmation. Never store bank credentials or collect card details in the app.

## What is implemented locally

- **Shop → Browse gadgets** links to `/shop/gadgets`, a manually transcribed public-catalogue snapshot with Afkit's own image URLs (the user confirmed photo permission). It has 87 distinct observed items, while Afkit reported 88; this discrepancy means the export is not proven complete. Items are inquiries only: the CEA WhatsApp link identifies the exact observed title and photo, and price, condition and availability must be checked before taking an order. When the API is unavailable it falls back to this static snapshot. It is not live inventory, checkout or a Merchant Center feed. Images remain hosted by Afkit and may disappear or change. Product IDs have not been supplied and image URLs are references, not stable SKUs. Do not promise product availability, delivery or pricing based on this snapshot.
- **Admin → Supplier review** is a browser-only workspace. Paste a markdown-rendered listing snapshot or the output of the optional collector. It keeps the original observed title including visible defects, marks missing stock as unknown, flags multiple prices rather than selecting one, and estimates prices under editable assumptions. It does not save, sync, publish, accept orders, contact Afkit or transfer money. Reloading clears the review. The existing live-mode app shell restricts this screen to the admin role in the browser; it is not server-side access control for stored data. Mock/demo mode intentionally bypasses that check, so do not enter confidential data on a shared or untrusted device or in a demo.
- An **experimental, manually invoked** browser collector, `node scripts/afkit-review-snapshot.mjs --permission-confirmed`, visits the public paginated listing and writes a review-only markdown snapshot under the git-ignored `supplier-reviews.local/` directory. It is intentionally **not scheduled** or connected to the storefront. The user has confirmed supplier authorization for automated access every 12 hours and photo use; respect that cadence and applicable terms when using the collector. Install this project's dependencies and the Playwright Chromium browser (`npx playwright install chromium`) in an approved environment. It uses an isolated browser without signed-in credentials, limits pages and requests to Afkit hosts, and fails without writing a snapshot if counts, page reads or duplicate-image checks look inconsistent. A matching count does not prove completeness, stable identity or current stock. The collector has only been tested against synthetic HTML, not the live site; the development environment could not make direct network connections to Afkit. It may need adjustment with supplier-approved documentation. Avoid repeatedly hammering the supplier.
- Pricing defaults are **illustrations only**, editable in the UI: 1.5% plus ₦100 estimated fee (conservatively ignoring the local fee cap/waiver), 3% reserve, 18% target net margin, ₦5,000 minimum estimated profit and rounding up to ₦100. The operator must enter other per-item costs or explicitly confirm zero before estimates appear. The tool prices using the larger of the target-margin and minimum-profit thresholds, after estimated costs, fee and reserve, with integer kobo and conservative rounding. It cannot guarantee profitability. International card fees, actual supplier cost, delivery, payment-fee tax, returns, tax obligations, disputes and other losses may differ. Paystack's [published Nigeria pricing](https://paystack.com/pricing) should be checked against CEA's actual contract and payment channels.
- An optional regular-price comparison shows a normal estimated offer floor of 10% net margin, with an 8% final exception available only after explicit per-item approval. The margins are editable but cannot fall below 10% normal or 8% exceptional; changing inputs resets the exception. Both floors still account for costs, fees, reserves and minimum profit. A regular price at or below a floor produces no discount for that tier, and a price below the target is flagged. Nothing automatically sends an offer or changes checkout. Never invent a higher original price, fake a sale, or use pressure or covert discounts. Contacting an abandoning shopper requires a lawful basis, transparent notice, an easy opt-out and Nigerian legal review; checkout details are not blanket marketing consent.

## Afkit questions to settle in writing

> Thanks again for agreeing to dropshipping. Could you share your partner/wholesale price list and stable SKU or variant IDs? Do you have an API or regularly updated CSV/JSON feed for prices, exact stock, product condition/defects, images and delivery costs, and may we automatically access it and reuse the descriptions on cea.ng and the product data and images on Google Merchant Center? Can you reserve a specific item and price while a customer checks out, and for how long? How should we submit paid orders securely, receive acceptance/rejection and tracking, and handle changed prices, cancellations, refunds, chargebacks, warranty claims, damaged products and out-of-stock orders? Which areas and delivery charges apply to our customers? Do you support a Paystack subaccount/split or do you prefer reconciled supplier invoices? Who is the support contact and what are your fulfillment timelines?

Also agree who is the retailer of record, when and how the supplier is paid, whether packages carry CEA branding, customer-data processing/security and retention, test orders, service levels, audit and dispute handling. Do not ask Afkit to send secret credentials through public chat; arrange a secure channel.

## Google Merchant Center and next code work

CEA's existing `/shop` and generated Merchant feed are for a small static **digital** catalogue; they cannot safely represent volatile used hardware. Do not append draft observations to that feed. To launch physical products, first implement a separate authoritative database and stable product/variant mapping, supplier sync health and fail-closed freshness, reviewed content rights, condition/defect details, physical product pages, checkout, stock reservation/rechecks, shipping, refunds, order/fulfillment lifecycle and reconciliation. The product feed must derive from the same authoritative price, availability, condition and variant shown on crawlable landing pages and at checkout. Configure shipping and returns truthfully; do not invent GTINs, warranties or strikethrough prices. See [Google's product data specification](https://support.google.com/merchants/answer/7052112?hl=en) and [landing page requirements](https://support.google.com/merchants/answer/4752265?hl=en). Nigeria appears in Google's [beta target-country list](https://support.google.com/merchants/answer/7101265?hl=en-GB); availability and eligibility should be checked in the actual Merchant Center account.

After Afkit provides integration details: implement an authenticated, bounded sync with per-run audit, freshness expiry and alerts; stable IDs and variant-specific stock; an admin approval/pause queue; a server-calculated quote and idempotent physical checkout; provider-verified payments and inventory/price checks; supplier order submission with idempotency, acknowledgement and retries; explicit cancellation/refund and manual exception queues; tracking notifications and privacy controls; reviewed payout and ledger reconciliation; and dynamic feed/landing-page parity tests. Run contract tests with the supplier's sandbox or explicit authorization, concurrent stock/price-change tests, webhook replay/failure tests, and real staged fulfillment before any public sales. This requires more than adding keys.

## Twelve-hour refresh implementation

The `Afkit catalogue refresh` GitHub Actions workflow is scheduled at 00:00 and 12:00 UTC, with a manual dispatch option and one run at a time. It requires the repository variable `AFKIT_AUTOMATION_AUTHORIZED` to be `true`. The first manually dispatched refresh succeeded in [run 43683750108](https://github.com/CyberElias-TechPros/lumina-studio/actions/runs/43683750108); verify the next scheduled run separately. The user has confirmed Afkit permission for photo use and automated site access every 12 hours; comply with its terms. The site currently reports 88 products while 87 cards were observed; the collector accepts at most a two-item and 3% discrepancy, rechecks the first and last nonempty pages, and refuses duplicates, large discrepancies, redirects or unstable first/last pages. This does not establish full completeness or prevent intermediate pages from changing. Products not present in an accepted observation are not advertised as available. The image URL is an observation reference, not a permanent SKU. Inspect every failure and supplier changes; do not repeatedly retry or bypass blocks.

The GitHub runner uses an isolated browser and allows only GET/HEAD requests to Afkit hosts. It sends an authenticated, bounded snapshot to the CEA API; it does not send customer information to Afkit. The API validates and atomically replaces the last snapshot only with a newer observation; untrusted or partial payloads are rejected. The storefront calls the API with no cache, shows an **estimated** CEA price when available, and hides prices once the observation is more than 18 hours old or the API is unavailable. It does not display observed quantities as stock guarantees. Unknown quantities remain unknown. Multiple supplier prices remain ambiguous and get no calculated price. No ordering, stock reservation, payment collection, supplier settlement or Merchant Center integration is added. Delivery is quoted separately. A 12-hour scrape cannot guarantee available stock or price; staff must confirm with Afkit and the customer before an order.

### Pricing defaults and configuration

The calculation treats the observed **retail** listing price as a provisional cost proxy, not as an agreed wholesale price: net margin target 18%, risk reserve 3%, estimated payment fee 1.5% + ₦100, other per-item costs ₦5,000, minimum estimated profit ₦5,000, rounded up to ₦100. This intentionally ignores the local fee cap/waiver and quotes delivery separately. It does not establish actual tax, payment fees, returns or profit; review for each product and fulfillment region. It may price CEA above Afkit and therefore may not be competitive. A two-price card is unpriced until resolved. Operators can change Worker variables, in integer units:

| Worker variable | Default | Unit |
| --- | ---: | --- |
| `AFKIT_MARGIN_BPS` | 1800 | basis points of sale price |
| `AFKIT_RESERVE_BPS` | 300 | basis points of sale price |
| `AFKIT_PAYMENT_FEE_BPS` | 150 | basis points of sale price |
| `AFKIT_FIXED_FEE_KOBO` | 10000 | kobo per transaction |
| `AFKIT_OTHER_COST_KOBO` | 500000 | kobo per item, excluding delivery quoted separately |
| `AFKIT_MIN_PROFIT_KOBO` | 500000 | kobo per item |
| `AFKIT_ROUND_KOBO` | 10000 | kobo rounding increment |

Combined percentages must total less than 10,000 basis points, and rounding must be positive. Invalid configuration causes the API to hide prices rather than guessing. Existing Worker environment variables override these defaults, so an already-configured `AFKIT_MARGIN_BPS` can retain a different target after deployment. Defaults are changeable server-side; the browser-only admin calculator does not configure these Worker variables or change storefront prices.

### Activation and verification

1. The user confirmed automated-access permission. Ask whether the public price is the price CEA will actually pay, and confirm delivery, returns, exact item identity and support process.
2. The refresh infrastructure and migration `0077_afkit_catalog_snapshot.sql` are deployed. Deploy subsequent pricing and frontend changes through the normal reviewed process. The migration adds a single D1 snapshot row, not orders or customer data. No sync credentials are in source control.
3. Generate a high-entropy, URL-safe secret (at least 32 characters) privately. Add the **same** value to the Worker secret `AFKIT_SYNC_TOKEN` and the GitHub Actions secret `AFKIT_SYNC_TOKEN`, using their secure settings/CLI; never paste it into chat, URLs, logs, `NEXT_PUBLIC_*`, or Git. Keep it separate from other credentials and rotate both copies if exposed. Optionally configure the Worker pricing variables above to match actual economics.
4. Only once authorization and configuration are complete, set repository Actions variable `AFKIT_AUTOMATION_AUTHORIZED=true` and run `Afkit catalogue refresh` manually. Verify the run succeeded, inspect the observed count against Afkit, compare a sample of exact titles/defects/prices/stock and verify ambiguous cards are unpriced. Then observe the next scheduled run. A skipped job, stale snapshot, failed collector or unavailable API is **not** a successful sync; prices will eventually disappear. Turn the variable off to stop further scheduled collection.
5. Continue handling inquiries manually and recheck Afkit before agreeing the final price and availability. Investigate supplier or Actions failures promptly. A scheduled GitHub workflow is best effort, not a guaranteed exact 12-hour service-level agreement.

The first manual production collection and authenticated upload succeeded; the next scheduled run and long-term reliability still need verification. The API and pricing guards are tested in isolation. No claim of profitable actual sales should be made before confirming real supplier costs, fees and fulfillment terms.
